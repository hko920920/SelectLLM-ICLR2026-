\section{Full Proofs and Formal Verification}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Product-frame construction}

Fix budget $B$ and let each canonical behavior be a vector $z\in\{A,B\}^B$. The public registry contains $2^B$ exact submitted copies of every $z$, for $4^B$ submitted IDs in total. A latent frame is indexed by $\omega\in\{A,B\}^B$. Define
\[
m_\omega(z)=\left|\{t:z_t=\omega_t\}\right|.
\]
Frame $\Phi_\omega$ partitions the $2^B$ public copies of $z$ into $r_\omega(z)=2^{m_\omega(z)}$ accountable roots, each containing $2^{B-m_\omega(z)}$ aliases. These are integer partitions of the same public leaves. Every frame contains
\[
\sum_z 2^{m_\omega(z)}=3^B
\]
roots. A uniform root prior therefore induces
\[
\Pr_\omega(F=z)=\frac{2^{m_\omega(z)}}{3^B}
=\prod_{t=1}^B
\begin{cases}
2/3,&z_t=\omega_t,\\
1/3,&z_t\neq\omega_t.
\end{cases}
\]
The candidate coordinates are independent, with $\Pr(F_t=\omega_t)=2/3$, although the submitted IDs and responses are identical in all $2^B$ frames.

For each coordinate $t$, the fixed pool contains two binary queries $q_{t,+}$ and $q_{t,-}$. With $c=1/4$, their channels are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
q_{t,+}:&\Pr(Y=1\mid F_t=A)=1,&\Pr(Y=1\mid F_t=B)=c,\\
q_{t,-}:&\Pr(Y=1\mid F_t=A)=1-c,&\Pr(Y=1\mid F_t=B)=0.
\end{array}
\]
Outcomes are conditionally independent across coordinates given $F$. If $\Pr(F_t=A)=2/3$, direct substitution in $I(F_t;Y)=H(Y)-H(Y\mid F_t)$ gives
\[
I(F_t;Y_{t,+})=0.540852=:I_{\rm hi},\qquad
I(F_t;Y_{t,-})=0.459148=:I_{\rm lo};
\]
the values reverse when the prior is $1/3$.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sequential}}

Place a uniform distribution over the $2^B$ frames. Until coordinate $t$ is queried, $\omega_t$ is independent of every observation from the other coordinates. Thus any frame-blind policy chooses the correct polarity on a fresh coordinate with average probability at most $1/2$, and obtains at most
\[
\tfrac12(I_{\rm hi}+I_{\rm lo})=0.5
\]
bits on its first query to that coordinate.

It remains to show that adaptivity and repeated queries do not improve this average bound. The prior entropy of one coordinate is $h(2/3)=0.918296$ bits. Even after the lower-information first query, its remaining entropy is at most
\[
h(2/3)-I_{\rm lo}=0.459148<0.5.
\]
Consequently, even a hypothetical follow-up that revealed all remaining uncertainty is worse than the average value of querying a fresh coordinate. A frame-aware policy also moves to a fresh coordinate because $h(2/3)-I_{\rm hi}=0.377444<I_{\rm hi}$. An exchange argument therefore transforms either optimal policy into one that queries each coordinate exactly once without reducing value.

The frame-aware value is $BI_{\rm hi}$, whereas the average frame-blind value is at most $B/2$. Hence the average regret over frames is at least
\[
B(I_{\rm hi}-1/2)=\frac{B}{2}(I_{\rm hi}-I_{\rm lo}),
\]
so at least one fixed frame attains the claimed lower bound. A policy that visits each coordinate once and independently randomizes its polarity obtains exactly $(I_{\rm hi}+I_{\rm lo})/2$ on every frame, proving tightness.

For Hamming loss, the posterior Bayes error is $1/12$ after the correct-polarity channel and $1/6$ after the wrong-polarity channel. Fair randomization gives $1/8$. An untouched coordinate has error $1/3$, so a fresh query reduces error by $5/24$; all error remaining after a first query is at most $1/6<5/24$. The same exchange argument applies, and the minimax excess is
\[
B(1/8-1/12)=B/24.
\]

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:amplification}}

Let $B\geq3$ be odd. The five canonical candidates are $A,B,C,D,E$, and the pool has $B$ good and $B$ bad binary items. On every good item their responses are $(0,0,1,1,1)$ and the reference is $0$. On bad item $t$ their responses are $(0,0,1,0,0)$, with reference $1$ for odd $t$ and $0$ for even $t$. The refinement adds four exact copies of $C$.

For Select-LLM's exact-match acquisition, write
\[
S(x)=x^2+(1-x)^2,
\]
where $x$ is posterior mass predicting one. After $s$ clean good labels, $A$ and $B$ have likelihood weight $a=e^{s/\tau}$ and $C,D,E$ have weight one. With $d=2a+3$, an unqueried good item has one-mass $3/d$, while a bad item has one-mass $1/d$. Since
\[
S(1/d)-S(3/d)=\frac{4(d-4)}{d^2}>0,
\]
every remaining good item has strictly lower acquisition score. The clean selector exhausts the good block, and $A,B$ remain selected.

In the refined registry, behavior $C$ has five of nine entries. Along the bad path, its aggregate posterior mass is $x=5/9$ after every even number of bad labels and
\[
x=\frac{5e^{1/\tau}}{5e^{1/\tau}+4}
\]
after every odd number, so $1/2<x<1$ for every finite $\tau>0$. A bad item has one-mass $x$. A good item groups $C$ with $D,E$ and has one-mass $(1+x)/2$. The bad item is strictly closer to one half because $x-1/2<x/2$ whenever $x<1$, and hence has lower $S$. Induction shows that the refined selector exhausts the disjoint bad block.

Across the complete $2B$-item pool, $A$ and $B$ outperform $C$ by
\[
\Delta_B=\frac{B-1}{2B}.
\]
After each odd bad label, $C$ has one more observed correct response than every non-$C$ candidate, so the selected behavior must be $C$. There are $(B+1)/2$ such steps. Even granting favorable tie-breaking on every even step, cumulative regret is at least
\[
\frac{B+1}{2}\Delta_B=\frac{B^2-1}{4B}.
\]
The final step is odd, giving terminal regret $\Delta_B$.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:factorization}}

Consider any allowed refinement and couple the clean and refined runs using the same policy randomization and reference randomness. Initially, fixed root masses and refinement-invariant summaries give identical root-level states. Assume the transcripts agree through step $t-1$. The selector's state is then the same by factorization, hence so is its conditional action kernel. Under the coupling it selects the same query and observes the same reference; refinement invariance produces the same updated root summaries and root decision. Induction gives identical complete transcripts and decisions almost surely, hence equal distributions. The argument applies to any acquisition objective---including uncertainty, diversity, or mutual information---only if its implemented state and action kernel satisfy the stated factorization.

\subsection{Executable verification}

The standard-library checker verifies the channel information values and Bayes errors; every integer root partition for $B=1,\ldots,6$; all deterministic polarity vectors against all frames for $B=1,\ldots,8$; and the amplification instance for $B\in\{3,5,11,51\}$ and $\tau\in\{.1,.5,1,3,10\}$. All assertions pass.
