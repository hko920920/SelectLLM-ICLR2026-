\section{Frame Identifiability and Sequential Amplification}
\label{sec:theory}

We ask whether labels can compensate for a missing frame in any response-only policy, whether a constant refinement can redirect a current selector, and how approximate root-level invariance controls the resulting transcript.

\paragraph{Response-compatible worlds.}
Let $O(E)=\{(e,a_e):e\in E\}$ be the public response view. Frames exposing the same $O(E)$ and label transcript are indistinguishable to a frame-blind policy: labels reveal useful behavior, not whether entries are independent roots. A fresh hidden-frame choice at every step makes this ambiguity accumulate.

For the construction below, let $V^\pi_{\Phi,B}$ be the expected information, in bits, acquired by policy $\pi$ about $B$ latent binary candidate coordinates, and let $V^*_{\Phi,B}$ be the optimum for a policy that knows $\Phi$. Define $I_{\mathrm{hi}}=0.540852$ and $I_{\mathrm{lo}}=0.459148$ bits.

\begin{theorem}[Tight cost of a missing evidence frame]
\label{thm:sequential}
For every integer budget $B\geq1$, there is one fixed public response registry, a pool with two binary queries per coordinate, and $2^B$ response-compatible frames such that every possibly randomized frame-blind policy has, on some fixed compatible frame,
\[
V^*_{\Phi,B}-V^\pi_{\Phi,B}
\geq \frac{B}{2}(I_{\mathrm{hi}}-I_{\mathrm{lo}})
=0.040852B\ \text{bits}.
\]
The bound is tight. The corresponding tight minimax excess coordinate-wise Hamming risk is $B/24$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
One latent frame bit per coordinate selects which of two query polarities yields $I_{\mathrm{hi}}$ instead of $I_{\mathrm{lo}}$. Under uniform compatible frames, an untouched bit is independent of prior observations, so a frame-blind policy chooses the informative polarity with probability at most one half. Revisiting cannot improve its minimax value. Averaging yields a fixed hard frame; independent randomization attains equality. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} gives the construction and $1/24$ Hamming-risk calculation.

The construction grows exponentially with $B$, proving necessity rather than practical registry size. A constant change can nevertheless be amplified by a concrete selector.

\begin{theorem}[Constant-registry full-path amplification]
\label{thm:amplification}
For every odd $B\geq3$ and finite Select-LLM temperature $\tau>0$, there is a pool with five canonical response behaviors and $2B$ items such that adding four exact aliases produces nine submitted entries, redirects all $B$ acquisitions to a disjoint query block, and changes cumulative regret from zero to at least
\[
\frac{B^2-1}{4B}=\Omega(B),
\]
while the terminal regret is $(B-1)/(2B)$. The two best canonical behaviors and all existing responses are unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof intuition.}
The clean registry always prefers a good query block. Four aliases move another behavior's mass above one half along an alternating-label path, making every bad query preferable and reinforcing the condition after each selection. Counting odd steps gives both regret bounds; Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} verifies them for every finite $\tau$.

This is mechanism-specific, not universal harm, and exact hashing blocks the literal construction. Section~\ref{sec:protocol} therefore tests response-distinct, quality-equivalent variants.

\begin{proposition}[Selector-agnostic root factorization]
\label{prop:factorization}
Suppose $\rho$ is authenticated, each root has fixed mass $w_r$, and at every step a selector's state, action kernel, and decision depend on entries only through root summaries $S_r$. If $S_r$ is invariant to an allowed within-root refinement, then the distribution of the complete query transcript and root decisions is invariant to that refinement.
\end{proposition}

Transcript induction proves the claim (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). It covers uncertainty, diversity, and mutual-information rules only when they actually factor through authenticated, refinement-invariant root summaries; a method label alone gives no protection. Exact quotienting handles unique response patterns, not response-distinct variants. Predictions also cannot authenticate $\rho$, so deployment still requires a declared canonical entry or fixed within-root mixture.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate transcript stability]
\label{prop:approximate-stability}
Consider two $B$-step root-level experiments with the same initial state and conditional next-state kernels $P_t(\cdot\mid h)$ and $Q_t(\cdot\mid h)$ on a common history space. If
\[
\sup_h \operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_t(\cdot\mid h),Q_t(\cdot\mid h)\right)\leq\varepsilon_t
\quad\text{for }t=1,\ldots,B,
\]
then their complete transcript laws satisfy
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(\mathcal L(T_B),\mathcal L(T'_B)\right)
\leq 1-\prod_{t=1}^B(1-\varepsilon_t)
\leq \min\!\left\{1,\sum_{t=1}^B\varepsilon_t\right\}.
\]
Consequently, for any transcript cost whose range has width at most $M$, the absolute difference in expected cost is at most $M\min\{1,\sum_t\varepsilon_t\}$.
\end{proposition}

A sequential maximal coupling proves the result (Appendix~\ref{app:v21-stability}). Proposition~\ref{prop:factorization} is the exact case $\varepsilon_t=0$. The bound also clarifies why small per-step representation errors can accumulate with annotation budget, while large path divergence need not imply large terminal cost: the latter additionally depends on the geometry of the competing roots.
