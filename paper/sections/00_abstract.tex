\begin{abstract}
Active model selection uses candidate outputs to decide which costly labels to acquire. In candidate-indexed mechanisms, assigning mass to every listed entry makes the roster itself an unreported experimental prior. We formalize the missing \emph{candidate evidence frame}: accountable roots, an entry-to-root map, and a root-mass rule. A natural exact version alias in a frozen 31-model roster changes all 1,500 paired acquisition paths without adding behavior, although label replacement is small and terminal harm is absent. We prove a tight annotation-budget-linear loss for frame-blind selectors and a constant-size refinement that redirects every query for an exact-match Select-LLM instance. Empirically, response-distinct aliases that preserve correctness coordinate-wise raise cumulative regret by 3.25/3.78/.85 on MedQA/GSM8K/OpenBookQA and terminal regret by 2.91/10.56 points on the first two. Fixed-query effects are zero, isolating acquisition mediation; order and tie audits preserve the result. Raw-text adapters provide a separate holdout-reference-free bridge: a development-informed, disjoint 13,000-row IMDB outcome yields $+.702$ [$.615,.788$] terminal points under the same acquisition/evidence view. A one-shot Yelp transfer is directionally positive but misses its predeclared materiality threshold. Other reference-free and cross-selector results are selective, and untargeted additions are mostly benign. We therefore establish targeted evidence-frame dependence, not universal instability, live-registry admission, or production compromise.
\end{abstract}
