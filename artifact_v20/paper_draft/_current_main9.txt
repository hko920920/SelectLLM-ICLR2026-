A BSTRACT
Active model selection uses candidate outputs to decide which costly examples to
label, so the supplied list silently defines a prior over the experiment. We show that
this representation is not invariant. In frozen responses from 31 models, restoring
one natural exact version alias changes all 1,500 paired acquisition paths despite
adding no behavior; registry-independent querying is unchanged. We formalize the
missing candidate evidence frame and prove a tight annotation-budget-linear lower
bound for selectors that see responses but not which entries deserve independent
mass. In a source-faithful exact-match Select-LLM specialization, our main publicreference intervention preserves correctness coordinate-wise, yet raises cumulative
regret by 3.25/3.78/.85 on MedQA/GSM8K/OpenBookQA and terminal regret by
2.91/10.56 points on the first two. Fixed-query effects are zero; 16 permutations and
all nine exact tie policies preserve the result. A reference-free decoupled-view test
passes terminal and active-minus-fixed gates on 3/8 untouched holdouts; learned
raw-input adapters likewise raise AG News terminal regret by .547 [.502, .593]
points. Two stricter learned, no-lookup, single-similarity confirmations change
99.8/100% of paths and raise cumulative regret on Banking77/20 Newsgroups by
.0409 [.0129, .0689]/.0822 [.0385, .1266], but both fail frozen .5-point terminal
gates. An official pairwise-judge selector passes on 2/6 datasets. Exact quotienting
misses non-identical aliases and untargeted additions are mostly benign. These
results establish targeted evidence-frame dependence, not universal instability
or production compromise; active evaluation should make candidate mass and
evidence views auditable.

We prove a tight budget-linear loss and construct four aliases that redirect every query. Exact-quality
public-reference interventions raise cumulative regret on three tasks and terminal regret by 2.91/10.56
points on MedQA/GSM8K; candidate order and nine exact tie policies preserve the result. Three

candidate agreement and reference feedback: cumulative regret rises, but terminal harm is null and
the frozen gate fails. An official LLM Selector audit passes corrected terminal gates on two of six
datasets; CODA and all negative cells bound the claim to selectable, not generic, degradation.
Our contributions are:
1. We define registry refinement and evidence-frame ambiguity, then show that a natural exact
alias rewrites every paired path across three tasks without adding behavior.
2. Executable refinements cause fresh-pool harm. Reference-free decoupled-view tests pass
3/8 untouched holdouts, learned raw-input adapters pass AG News, and a pairwise-judge
selector passes 2/6 datasets. A same-similarity Banking77 test changes paths and cumulative
cost but fails its terminal gate. Fixed-query effects are zero throughout.
3. We prove a tight budget-linear lower bound and construct four aliases that redirect all B
queries and cause Ω(B) cumulative regret.
4. Exact quotienting misses the main aliases; covers and clone-robust weights trade suppression
against clean selection. Untargeted additions are mostly benign.
We do not claim that every active selector is vulnerable, that a natural alias is adversarial, that every
refinement is harmful, or that similarity reveals ownership. The resulting reliability requirement is
narrower: an active evaluation should either bind submitted entries to a declared candidate evidence
frame or qualify conclusions that depend on unaudited entry multiplicity.

Active selection. Let X = {x1 , . . . , xN } be an unlabeled evaluation pool and E = {e1 , . . . , eH }
the submitted candidate entries. Entry e exposes response vector ae = (ae (x1 ), . . . , ae (xN )) and
has oracle utility vector ue , binary per-example correctness in our experiments. An active policy π
sequentially acquires B references. After step t, its transcript contains the queried indices, acquired
references, and selected entries; using an oracle root map, we evaluate the corresponding root-level
choices rb1 , . . . , rbt .
Definition 1 (Candidate evidence frame). A frame Φ = (R, ρ, w) maps submitted entries to accountable candidate roots through ρ : E → R and assigns root mass wr . A root is the unit that the
evaluation claim intends to count once; it need not be observable or cryptographically authenticated.
Two frames are response-compatible when they expose the same entry identifiers and response vectors.
Definition 2 (Registry refinement). A refinement of root r adds entries mapped to r without modifying
existing entries. It is exact when an added response vector copies its parent and quality-equivalent
when its oracle utility vector equals the parent’s coordinate-wise, even if their responses differ.
Root-level refinement integrity requires a declared within-root rule under which such representation
changes do not silently redefine the evaluation claim.
P
For example, a uniform per-entry mechanism induces the empirical measure µE = |E|−1 e∈E δae .
Adding k entries changes one root’s represented mass without adding a root; candidate-consensus
mechanisms can change analogously because consensus is computed over listed entries. A policy is
frame-blind when it observes responses and acquired references but not authenticated ρ or w.

We do not claim the first exact-duplicate preprocessor, sequential replication or clone effect, registry
effect, multiplicity attack, clone-proof weight, evidence-backed quotient, or lineage system. Our
object is the shared-label sequential boundary: what predictions justify about response-distinct
candidate units, how latent same-root multiplicity alters acquired reusable labels, and what root-level
decision cost follows. Lineage is useful missing input, not a selection policy supplied here.
Natural refinement versus attack. Our natural anchor compares the original frozen roster with
the exact-response quotient that removes its later-index version alias. It attributes neither common
control outside the evaluation frame nor adversarial intent to the provider. It asks only whether a
behaviorally redundant listed entry changes the active experiment. The adversarial tiers below are
separate interventions that test whether an actor controlling one root can exploit the same entry-mass
channel.

Proposition 1 (Selector-agnostic root factorization). Suppose ρ is authenticated, each root has

Closest conceptually, strategic-replication bandits let known agents register duplicate arms, yielding
linear regret, and motivate agent-first hierarchical defenses (Shin et al., 2022; 2025). They observe
arm-local rewards under supplied ownership; here one label scores every entry, roots are latent, and
the object includes terminal root choice. Active evaluation tests perturbed, separately ranked clones;
UNREAL and PUTSC quotient redundant committees or candidates (Lanctot et al., 2026; Nguyen
et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2020). We claim neither the first sequential replication nor clone effect. Our
remaining object is shared-label exact-utility refinement with fixed-query mediation to root regret
(Table 1); correlation-aware selection fixes an ensemble (Turkmen et al., 2026).
Static registry attacks change ranks, models, matches, or descriptions, not future labels (Hays
et al., 2026; Suri et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2026). Dilution priors, clone
weights, quotients, and lineage constrain static influence or identify roots (George, 2010; Berriaud
& Wattenhofer, 2026b; Procaccia et al., 2025; Burnat & Davidson, 2026; Shang et al., 2026); ours
changes which reusable labels are acquired, which post-hoc quotienting cannot recover.
