# Theory upgrade plan: quotient characterization and approximate refinement invariance

Date: 2026-08-14
Status: outcome-independent mathematical work item for the next manuscript version.

## Motivation

The current paper gives a sufficient root-factorization proposition. Review A correctly notes that the strongest amplification theorem is Select-LLM and exact-match specific. The next version should separate the universal invariance statement from selector-specific lower bounds.

## Theorem A: quotient characterization

Let `E` be an admitted entry registry, `rho:E->R` an authenticated root map, and `~rho` the equivalence relation generated by allowed within-root refinements. Let a sequential selector be specified by measurable state-update and action kernels over the public registry and acquired transcript.

**Claim.** The complete transcript-and-root-decision law is invariant under every allowed within-root refinement if and only if the selector descends to the authenticated quotient: there exists a kernel on equivalence classes `E / ~rho` whose pullback equals the original selector on every registry and transcript.

### Proof skeleton

- Sufficiency: couple two refined registries through the common quotient. Equal quotient states imply equal action and update kernels at step zero; induction gives equal transcript laws at every budget.
- Necessity: invariance makes the action/update law constant on each equivalence class. Define the quotient kernel by choosing any representative. Invariance makes this definition well-defined; measurability follows from the quotient sigma-algebra.

This is an if-and-only-if statement. It does not depend on Select-LLM, exact match, Bayesian updating, or a particular acquisition objective.

## Theorem B: approximate transcript stability

Consider two refinements coupled under a common authenticated root frame. Suppose that, conditional on every matched history through step `t-1`, the total-variation distance between their next action/update kernels is at most `epsilon_t`.

**Claim.** For budget `B`,

`TV(Law(T_B), Law(T'_B)) <= sum_{t=1}^B epsilon_t`.

For any terminal or cumulative cost `C` with range at most `M`,

`|E[C(T_B)] - E[C(T'_B)]| <= M * min(1, sum_t epsilon_t)`.

### Proof skeleton

Use maximal coupling at each matched step. The probability that the coupled transcripts first separate at step `t` is at most `epsilon_t`; a union/telescoping bound gives the transcript result. The bounded-cost result follows from the variational characterization of total variation.

## Corollaries to state explicitly

1. Exact authenticated root canonicalization gives every `epsilon_t=0` and therefore bitwise refinement invariance.
2. Response-only soft deduplication can be evaluated by its induced per-step `epsilon_t`; similarity scores alone do not authenticate roots.
3. An approximate root summary with a known stability modulus yields a quantitative regret bound rather than an all-or-nothing defense claim.
4. Entry-indexed mass violates the quotient condition whenever an allowed refinement changes posterior mass or an acquisition group.
